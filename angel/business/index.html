Why it could fit

Built around
the Guild.

Angel's leadership has emphasized efficient Guild growth, stronger member engagement and expansion within available means. A wireless partnership is worth pursuing when it measurably improves household value and adds contribution after every cost.

The first commercial hypothesis is a competitive primary-line service with a modest recurring Guild credit. A larger household bundle with membership included could follow if actual margins support it.

Proposed roles

The right work.

01 / ANGEL

The brand and the member.

Brand, creative, offer approval, Guild entitlements, member communications and content governance.

02 / SERVICE PARTNERS

The wireless operation.

A named provider and merchant, contracted network access, activation, billing, refunds, service care and applicable obligations.

03 / FREEMAN SIGNAL

Bring it together.

Partner sourcing, implementation, integration, benefit reconciliation, reporting, operating oversight and a negotiated path to internalize functions.

A pilot worth
learning from.

Begin with operating validation, then a bounded paid cohort. Compare the branded offer with a credible referral alternative and a holdout. Track paid activation, number transfer outcomes, care cost, retained contribution and incremental Guild retention.

Current-member discounts, annual prepaid terms, acquisition costs, churn, launch spending and media opportunity cost all belong in the model. A loyal audience is a distribution opportunity; willingness to switch remains to be proven.

No provider, rate, network right, final price or launch agreement has been established. These previews demonstrate the brand and proposition.
