A proposed launch path

Build the audience.
Bring commitment forward.

Start with a free, nonbinding wireless interest list. Learn how many households, eligible lines and service areas are real prospects before asking anyone to transfer a number. A waitlist signals interest; paid, retained activations prove demand.

Test a separate, voluntary annual Guild offer with eligible members: monthly members can move to annual; existing annual members could buy a 12-month extension after their current paid-through date, if Angel supports that mechanism. New members can buy a real Guild membership with immediate entertainment value. No duplicate membership or forfeited prepaid time.

One possible incentive is a one-time wireless credit after eligible activation. Its amount and funding would have to pass the margin test. A Guild purchase must have clear standalone value even if wireless never launches. Priority is an invitation order, not guaranteed coverage, price or availability.

At an illustrative $20 monthly baseline, those 1,000 monthly members would pay $240,000 over 12 months if all stayed. Converting them to $180 annual collects $160,000 more in month one, but gives up $60,000 of full-year billings against that no-churn baseline. Retention may change the outcome; it has to be measured.

Additional paid phone lines could add wireless contribution even after the household credit reaches its cap. That depends on service revenue less network, support and other line costs. No per-line contribution is established yet.

Annual extensions accelerate cash; they do not create a second member or immediate incremental earned revenue. The $90,000 from new households is only incremental to the extent a holdout shows those households would not have joined anyway. Subtract incentives, fees, refunds, support and acquisition costs before estimating contribution.

For example, a proposed $25 activation credit for all 4,500 participating households would cost up to $112,500 if all activated. It is separate from the recurring Guild credit, and neither incentive should be promised until the combined economics work.

Angel already supports monthly-to-annual changes for web memberships. Early annual extensions are a new proposal, not a verified existing feature. Third-party billing requires its own supported process. Begin with web-billed members and reconcile exact paid-through dates.

A pilot worth
learning from.

Begin with operating validation, then a bounded paid cohort. Compare the branded offer with a credible referral alternative and a holdout. Track paid activation, number transfer outcomes, care cost, retained contribution and incremental Guild retention.

Current-member discounts, annual prepaid terms, acquisition costs, churn, launch spending and media opportunity cost all belong in the model. A loyal audience is a distribution opportunity; willingness to switch remains to be proven.

No provider, rate, network right, final price or launch agreement has been established. These previews demonstrate the brand and proposition.
