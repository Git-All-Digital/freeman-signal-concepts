A household bill.
A recurring business.

Win a household's existing wireless spend with a competitive family bundle, rather than asking it to switch for a token credit. More retained lines create more recurring contribution.

After the fourth line, each additional line adds $20 of monthly revenue. At the illustrated $14 variable cost, that is $6 of contribution per extra line before fixed costs.

The four-line $90 offer needs an all-in variable cost no higher than about $14.19 per line to leave $20 per household before fixed costs.

At four lines, a $14 cost leaves $20.75 per household per month; $18 leaves only $4.75; $22 loses $11.25. A quote above the cost ceiling means renegotiating or redesigning the offer, not pretending a higher volume solves a loss.

The Guild provision uses the $159 annual retail reference divided by 12 as a conservative placeholder. It is not an agreed wholesale charge. For an existing member, model lost external Guild contribution or a prepaid wireless credit consistently; do not deduct the same membership benefit twice. For a new member, replace the placeholder with actual incremental Guild economics.

Activation incentives, acquisition, refunds, taxes, handset subsidies, launch costs, overhead and ramp timing still need separate treatment. This steady-state result is not cash flow or profit. Target eligible unique US households, not Angel's global member count.

Market check: Visible currently advertises a $19 monthly holiday promotion, and US Mobile advertises lower first-year annual rates. This offer cannot claim universal cheapest pricing or equivalent coverage/data. Test the actual complete offer against alternatives plus a separately purchased Guild membership.

A proposed launch path

Build the audience.
Bring commitment forward.

Start with a free, nonbinding wireless interest list. Learn how many households, eligible lines and service areas are real prospects before asking anyone to transfer a number. A waitlist signals interest; paid, retained activations prove demand.

Test a separate, voluntary annual Guild offer with eligible members: monthly members can move to annual; existing annual members could buy a 12-month extension after their current paid-through date, if Angel supports that mechanism. New members can buy a real Guild membership with immediate entertainment value. No duplicate membership or forfeited prepaid time.

One possible incentive is a one-time wireless credit after eligible activation. Its amount and funding would have to pass the margin test. A Guild purchase must have clear standalone value even if wireless never launches. Priority is an invitation order, not guaranteed coverage, price or availability.

At an illustrative $20 monthly baseline, those 1,000 monthly members would pay $240,000 over 12 months if all stayed. Converting them to $159 annual collects $139,000 more in month one, but gives up $81,000 of full-year billings against that no-churn baseline. Retention may change the outcome; it has to be measured.

Additional paid phone lines add service revenue at the published proposed tiers. Each extra line after four adds $20 monthly revenue; contribution depends on the actual all-in line cost shown above.

Annual extensions accelerate cash; they do not create a second member or immediate incremental earned revenue. The $79,500 from new households is only incremental to the extent a holdout shows those households would not have joined anyway. Subtract incentives, fees, refunds, support and acquisition costs before estimating contribution.

For example, a proposed $25 activation credit for all 4,500 participating households would cost up to $112,500 if all activated. It is separate from the family membership provision or its prepaid-term substitution credit. Both costs belong in the combined economics.

Angel already supports monthly-to-annual changes for web memberships. Early annual extensions are a new proposal, not a verified existing feature. Third-party billing requires its own supported process. Begin with web-billed members and reconcile exact paid-through dates.
